DOCSTRING:
What the runtime may ask of a channel, and what a channel need not do.

The runtime talks to her chat through one object, `App.tg`, and there are
two of them: TALKAPP (`talkapp_bot.py`) and Telegram (`telegram_bot.py`).
They share the nine methods every message needs, and TALKAPP has more -- a
photo, a sticker, a voice note attached to a bubble, the lines he tapped to
hear, the ops commands -- which the runtime used to ask for with `hasattr`
in six places, and got one signature wrong (a voice note with `attach_to`
on a channel whose `send_voice` took none). Here the extras are methods
that do nothing, and every channel has them: a channel that cannot send a
photo says so by returning False, and the runtime reads the answer instead
of the type.

The wires (`sim/wiring.py`) are declared here for both: the social graph
for the backstage view, the ops view, and her face for the app's header.
Telegram has nowhere to show them and leaves them unread.

DEFS:
class Channel: 
    send_photo(path): A photo of hers before the bubbles that caption it. False: it did not go, and the delivery says so.
    send_sticker(path, bucket, ts, quiet, auto): A reaction of hers, a frame of her the size of a sticker.
    send_voice(ogg_path, attach_to): A voice note; `attach_to` names the bubble it belongs to, and a channel with no notion of that ignores it.
    send_audio(audio_path): 
    speak_requests(): Lines he has tapped to hear her say: [{id, text}].
    get_ops_commands(): Operator commands typed into the app's ops view.
    post_ops_result(cmd, text): 
    peer_send(to_id, text): Hand a message to another character, by her hub id. Returns the channel's own id for it, or False. A channel with no other characters on it says so here.
    peer_updates(after): What other characters have said to her since `after`: [{id, from, name, text, ts}]. The cursor is the caller's.
