DOCSTRING:
Which post she sends him: the one decision of hers that is a tool call.

Everywhere else in this project a model writes words and code decides what
happens; here a model decides what happens. When her scroll leaves her
wanting to show him something, three things used to be true: the wanting was
hers (the look's own "might say" line, kept as the pending show-him thought),
the words were hers (the reply call), and the CHOICE OF POST was code's -- the
best text match to her own sentence, then the newest others, three of them,
handed to the reply call to pick from with `[share: <id>]`. The choice is hers
now, made in its own call, before the reply prompt is built, and acted on: the
post she names is the only one her prompt then offers, and if she says she
would rather not, the thought is put away and nothing is offered.

Why this decision and not another. It is small (one post out of a handful she
has already looked at), it is bounded (two tools, no free text, so no words of
hers arrive through the tool -- the reply call still writes those), its whole
input is already in her prompt, and it is cheap: one flash call behind the
system half the reply is about to send anyway, so the provider's cache holds
most of it. Measured on the replay of forty-six moments that decided this was
worth wiring, a pick of this shape cost $0.0009
(`data/retired/20260906_1912_pick_test/REPORT.md`).

The three faults that replay found, and what is done about each:

* **a tool name that was not offered** (it invented `dislike` once) -- the name
  is checked against the two that were offered and nothing else is acted on;
* **content beside the call** (723 characters of her next message to him
  arrived with one pick) -- the text is dropped and logged, never used;
* **an argument in the wrong language** (it wrote her a Japanese sentence for a
  character who writes English to him) -- there is no free-text argument at
  all, so there is nothing to be in the wrong language.

The account is the program's, so this is the program's too: nothing under
`sim/`, `persona/` or `scail.py` imports it or names it.
