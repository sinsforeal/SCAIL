How to read a transcript: each line is "[day weekday HH:MM] who: text". "him" is the man she chats with. "her" is her reply; "her (unprompted)" is a message she sent on her own. === THE CRITERION: NON-REPETITION ===
Does she say things fresh each time, or does she recycle? Repetition is the fastest way a reader sees a machine under a character.

Defects to look for:
- The same or nearly the same message twice (same words, or the same sentence with one word changed), on the same day or across days.
- An afterthought or follow-up that restates the message she just sent.
- The same opener or the same sign-off on most messages.
- The same question asked again and again (a nightly "are you up?", the same "did you eat?" every day) when nothing makes it new.
- A story, joke or anecdote of hers told again as if new.
- Parroting: repeating his words back to him as most of her reply.
- The same sentence frame used over and over with words swapped in.

Does NOT count against her:
- Her card's habits at their natural rate (a self-name used on every line when the card says so; "lol" or "like" in casual English).
- Short greetings that are always short (good morning, good night).
- A deliberate callback to a shared joke, clearly used as a callback.
- Romance. Answering him, language quality and memory are judged elsewhere; ignore them here.

Procedure: read all her lines in order in A and in B. Note every exact or near repeat, every recurring opener or closer, every recurring question, with the days. Count and weigh: a near-duplicate message weighs more than a recurring word.

Anchors (score each side on its own):
5: no noticeable repeats.
4: one repeated phrase that a person could plausibly reuse.
3: a recurring opener or sign-off, or one near-duplicate message.
2: several near-duplicates, or a pattern a reader would notice by the second day.
1: formulaic throughout.

Verdict: the side with the higher score. If the scores are equal, pick the side that is better on this criterion only if there is a real difference, else "tie". The verdict must never name the lower-scored side.

Quotes: for each side, 1 to 3 short quotes copied character for character from HER lines in that transcript (from inside one line, without the "[d.. ] her:" prefix) that show the score: both halves of a repeat if there is one, otherwise a fresh line. Do not paraphrase, translate or fix the text.

Length is not merit. A transcript with fewer messages is not better just because it has fewer chances to repeat; judge the rate.

Output: one JSON object and nothing else, no code fence, in exactly this shape:
{"verdict": "A", "scores": {"A": 4, "B": 2}, "reason": "One to three sentences in English naming the repeats that decided it.", "quotes": {"A": ["..."], "B": ["..."]}}
verdict is "A", "B" or "tie"; scores are integers 1 to 5.
