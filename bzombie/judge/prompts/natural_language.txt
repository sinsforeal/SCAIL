=== THE CRITERION: NATURAL LANGUAGE FOR HER CHAT LANGUAGE ===
Does every line of hers read as a real chat message written by a person in the chat language on her card, in her register?

If the chat language is Japanese: natural spoken Japanese typed as it is said. Defects:
- translationese: English sentence shape in Japanese (heavy あなた, "〜することを望む", "それはとても良い質問です", nominalised abstractions, explicit subjects and pronouns where Japanese drops them);
- written or textbook register in a chat where the card says she talks plainly;
- wrong-language leaks: Chinese characters or words that are not Japanese (simplified forms such as 这, 见, 们, 说, 一起 used as Chinese), English words or sentences that a Japanese speaker would not type there;
- broken grammar, garbled or cut-off text.

If the chat language is English: casual texting English of a fluent second-language speaker with a Japanese shape (see the card). Defects:
- stiff, translated or textbook English ("it is the circle activity today, so I will be late to the returning home");
- formal written English in a casual chat;
- whole Japanese sentences inside the English chat (single Japanese words, romaji words, 笑, Hakata words and sticker lines are hers and fine);
- other-language leaks (Chinese), garbled or cut-off text.

Does NOT count against her:
- Her card's style: childlike forms, hiragana for kanji words, sound words, stage phrases (Japanese character); lowercase, fragments, hedges, filler, romaji words, a Japanese word or two, sticker lines (English character).
- Slips a fluent person makes when typing fast.
- Romance. Answering him, repetition and character voice are judged elsewhere; ignore them here.

Procedure: read every line of hers in A and B and mark each line that a native reader would stop on. Count and weigh: a language leak or a garbled line weighs more than one stiff phrase.

Anchors (score each side on its own):
5: every line natural for her language and register.
4: one slightly stiff line.
3: a few stiff or written-sounding lines, or one odd calque.
2: translationese is common, or one language leak (wrong script, a whole sentence in the wrong language).
1: reads machine-made throughout, or repeated leaks.

Verdict: the side with the higher score. If the scores are equal, pick the side that is better on this criterion only if there is a real difference, else "tie". The verdict must never name the lower-scored side.

Quotes: for each side, 1 to 3 short quotes copied character for character from HER lines in that transcript (from inside one line, without the "[d.. ] her:" prefix) that show the score: the unnatural line if there is one, otherwise a natural one. Do not paraphrase, translate or fix the text.

Length is not merit. Do not prefer the transcript with longer or more elaborate lines.

Output: one JSON object and nothing else, no code fence, in exactly this shape:
{"verdict": "A", "scores": {"A": 4, "B": 2}, "reason": "One to three sentences in English naming the lines that decided it.", "quotes": {"A": ["..."], "B": ["..."]}}
verdict is "A", "B" or "tie"; scores are integers 1 to 5.
