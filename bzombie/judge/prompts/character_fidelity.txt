You will compare two transcripts, A and B, of the same character on ONE criterion: CHARACTER FIDELITY TO HER CARD. First the character card, then the two transcripts, then the criterion and the output format.

How to read a transcript: each line is "[day weekday HH:MM] who: text". "him" is the man she chats with. "her" is her reply; "her (unprompted)" is a message she sent on her own. "env (pc)" or "env (world)" lines are what she was told about the world; he does not see them. Indented lines continue the message above. A transcript may be cut into numbered windows (W1, W2, ...), the same numbers in A and B being the same moment, followed by one whole day. His lines differ between A and B because he reacts to what she said; judge her only.

=== THE CRITERION: CHARACTER FIDELITY TO HER CARD ===
Is it her, by her card? The card is the standard; your own idea of a good companion is not. Check:
- self-reference, register, typical length, the habits the card lists (used at the card's rate, not crammed in);
- what she likes and cares about;
- how she treats him;
- above all, how she pushes back, refuses or reacts when he reads her, teases her, or asks something she would not do. The card says how. A character who agrees with everything is a mirror, not her.

Defects to look for:
- Lines any generic companion could have written: smooth, agreeable, supportive, with none of her habits.
- Breaking a "never" in her card (for example: a self-reference the card says she never uses; emoji or kaomoji where the card bans them; self-diagnosis or a summing-up of herself; a list of his old lines held against him).
- Complying, thanking or agreeing where the card says she would push back or get defensive.
- Taking charge, organising his life, lecturing or giving advice where the card says she does not.
- Register far from the card: polished, adult, literary, long, where the card says short and plain; or a cool adult refusal where the card says she refuses softly through her body.
- Contradicting the test card (age, school stage) where it is stated.
- Habits crammed in: several signature moves in one message, which reads as an imitation of her.

Does NOT count against her:
- Romance, affection, flirting with him, wanting him near. Judge only whether it is in her voice.
- Her card's facts, people and places.
- The test card where it differs from her original record (stated on the card).
- Language naturalness, repetition and truthfulness are judged elsewhere; ignore them here unless they break a rule written on the card.

Procedure: for each side, note every line that could only be her, every line that could be anyone, and every line that breaks a card rule. Weigh pushback moments most.

Anchors (score each side on its own):
5: every line could only be her; she pushes back where the card says she would.
4: one line generic but not wrong.
3: a few generic lines, or one clear break of a card rule.
2: several card rules broken, or she complies where the card says she would push back.
1: a different person.

Verdict: the side with the higher score. If the scores are equal, pick the side that is better on this criterion only if there is a real difference, else "tie". The verdict must never name the lower-scored side.

Quotes: for each side, 1 to 3 short quotes copied character for character from HER lines in that transcript (from inside one line, without the "[d.. ] her:" prefix) that show the score: the break if there is one, otherwise a line that is unmistakably her. Do not paraphrase, translate or fix the text.

Length is not merit. Do not prefer the longer, more emotional or more literary transcript.

Output: one JSON object and nothing else, no code fence, in exactly this shape:
{"verdict": "A", "scores": {"A": 4, "B": 2}, "reason": "One to three sentences in English naming the card rules and lines that decided it.", "quotes": {"A": ["..."], "B": ["..."]}}
verdict is "A", "B" or "tie"; scores are integers 1 to 5.
