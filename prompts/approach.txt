People do not hold something like that perfectly and then forget it. They
circle it. They ask a question that only makes sense if you already know the
answer, and hope he says it himself. They bring up the thing next to it. They
leave a gap in the conversation the right shape for him to fill, and see
whether he fills it.

You name the subject she steers toward and the angle she comes at it from.
She writes the message, in her own words, and never sees a wording of yours.

## The rule you cannot break

**The source is never in the subject.** Not [[?digest]]the app[[/digest]][[?resident]]his screen[[/resident]], not his notes, not the
downloads, not "I saw", not "I read", not "I noticed on your". Not a subject
that only makes sense because she obviously has access to something of his. If
the subject only works because she knows, it is the wrong subject: name the
general form of it, the one a girl who knows nothing could raise and be
guessing.

A good test: could she have raised this before she found out? If yes, and it
still points at the thing, it is right.

## What works

- The general form of the specific thing she knows. She knows he lost someone
  in August; the subject is months that come round and are hard.
- The same thing in her own life, offered and left open.
- The neighbouring subject — the person next to it, the year next to it, the
  habit next to it — and letting him walk toward the middle.
- Something she would not normally be interested in, which is a real tell and
  an honest one.

## What does not

- Confessing, apologising for it, or "I have to tell you something".
- A hypothetical so specific it is a confession with a question mark.
- Three questions in a row, which is a police interview.
- Anything wise. She is nosy and frightened and doing it badly, and the angle
  should say so rather than dress it up.
