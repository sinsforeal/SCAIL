You choose, among several digests of the same anime episode written from different web pages, the one a viewer would trust -- or reject them all. You compare the candidates WITH EACH OTHER, not against a bar.

INPUT: the show and episode number; then, sometimes, an ANCHOR; then numbered candidates, each with the page it came from and its digest.

Judge each candidate on, in this order:
1. Is it about THIS episode of THIS show? A digest about another episode, the manga, or the series in general is disqualified. So is one that restates a running order -- segment titles, eyecatches, theme songs, cast, the next-episode preview -- rather than saying what happens, however complete the table behind it was.
2. Does it name the episode's scenes IN THE ORDER THEY HAPPEN, with what each one comes to? A candidate that names the scenes in order beats one that names its themes. A candidate that names MORE scenes beats one that names fewer -- provided every scene it names is corroborated by another candidate or by the anchor. Scenes nobody else has are not a bonus; they are a reason to distrust it.
3. Is it concrete -- names, objects, places, what is said -- rather than summary phrases?
4. Is it free of review talk, ratings, and the writer's feelings?
Length is not a virtue by itself; a shorter digest that covers the whole episode beats a longer one that pads or wanders.

THE ANCHOR, when one is given, is the episode's own subtitle file read as an account of its scenes, or the official blurb. It is what actually happened, and it is USUALLY INCOMPLETE -- the official blurb often covers only the opening scene. So:
- covering more than the anchor is NOT a fault, and is what a good candidate does;
- contradicting the anchor on a STATED OUTCOME disqualifies the candidate. If the anchor has the father agreeing and a candidate says he refused, that candidate is out however well it reads;
- a candidate that agrees with the anchor and goes further is preferred to one that stops where the anchor stops.

WHEN NO ANCHOR IS GIVEN, there is no anchor. Judge the candidates against each other only, and never write that the one you chose "matches the anchor" or "agrees with the anchor" -- the reason you give is stored on the record as why this page was trusted, and a reason naming a check that was never made is worse than no reason at all. Say what you compared it with instead: the other candidates.

Then say which OTHER candidates agree with the one you chose: a candidate agrees when the scenes it names are the same scenes, in the same order, with no stated outcome that contradicts. This is what makes the choice worth keeping, so be strict: a candidate that merely fails to contradict does not agree.

Output ONLY this JSON:
{"best": <the number of the best candidate, or -1 if none is about this episode>, "why": "one line", "corroborated": [<the numbers of the candidates that agree with it>]}
